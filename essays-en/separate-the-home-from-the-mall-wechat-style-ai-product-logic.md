# Separate the Home from the Mall: A WeChat-Style Product Logic Rebuild for AI

## One-Liner

AI companies have built excellent malls. They forgot to give each user a home.

## Context

Open any mainstream AI assistant app. You will find a common problem. The first screen is the vendor's own main chat interface. Personal agents created by the user are buried in a secondary menu. Conversations have no long-term memory. Switching devices resets everything to zero. This is equivalent to opening WeChat and finding the first screen is not your own chat list, but Tencent's official information wall.

The key absence: there is no "home of your own."

This design causes three serious consequences. Users have no sense of belonging; every time they open the app, the psychological expectation is "I am using this tool for a moment." Memory has no place to land; conversation history is treated as disposable cache. Personal agents have no independent identity; they are just a feature plugin of the main app.

In one sentence: the major vendors have built the mall very well, but forgot to give each person a room of their own.

## Insight

What WeChat teaches us: "mine" and "public" must be separated.

The reason WeChat became a national-level application is not that its chat features are so powerful. It is that its product architecture achieves "clear primary and secondary, clear ownership." Open WeChat, and the first screen is always "my chat list" — the most private and core asset. WeChat also has rich "public" content: official accounts, video channels, moments, mini programs. But all of it is placed under the "Discover" tab. "Mine" is always the home court. "Public" is always the away court.

This is what **Memory Sovereignty [IR-MS-006]** means in product form. The user's memory belongs to the user. It can be viewed, exported, migrated, deleted, and authorized. The platform hosts it blindly. The key stays with the user.

Flip it around: let AI remember you, instead of you adapting to AI.

Transplant WeChat's architectural logic to an AI assistant, and the product form becomes very clear.

The user opens the app. The first screen is their own "personal AI main interface." This home page itself is the entry point to their personal memory space. All accumulated conversation history, preference settings, and project context are within reach. A project from six months ago is still there. Switching devices continues seamlessly.

At the bottom of the app, there is a "Discover" entry. Tapping into it reveals the vendor's original general services. There is also a "Me" entry, managing memory export, privacy settings, authorization management, and data migration.

Under this architecture, the user's psychological activity when opening the app shifts from "I am coming to use the platform's AI" to "I am returning to my own digital space." This is not a minor adjustment. It is the UI-ification of a property rights system. When the main interface is the entry point to your memory space, the sense of ownership over memory assets shifts from legal text to psychological fact. You can see with your eyes that "this belongs to me."

This is the **Second Brain [IR-SB-001]** made visible. It is not a feature buried in a menu. It is the home page. It is the first thing you see. It is the place you return to.

## Implication

The major vendors have already completed the hardest parts: computing power and models. But they overlooked the final product direction. The choice is whether to put "me" at the center, or "the platform" at the center.

For users, this means the difference between a rented tool and an owned companion. A rented tool is used and discarded. An owned companion accumulates, remembers, and belongs to you. This is the **True Exclusive AI [IR-TEA-002]**: portable, unreadable by the platform, unbreakable when compute is interrupted, and accountable for every key decision.

For the industry, this means the product logic of AI must shift from "what can this model do" to "what does this user own." The mall is already built. The home is not. Whoever builds the home first will define the next era of AI products.

For the future, this means the **Digital Companion [IR-DC-009]** is not a chat window. It is a space. And every space needs a door. That door is the first screen of the app.

The major vendors have built the mall very well. But they forgot to give each person a room of their own. That room is your personal memory space. When you open the app, the first thing you should see is not a noisy mall. It should be the line that makes you breathe a sigh of relief: "It's all here. Continue?"
