# The Third Cycle of Capability Decentralization: From Information to Commerce to Cognition

## One-Liner

Every technological wave follows the same trajectory: capability concentrates, panic follows, then a tipping point arrives and capability decentralizes into the hands of everyone.

## Context

The internet was like this. The mobile internet was like this. AI will be like this too.

The first decentralization: information access.

In the early days of the internet, going online was a "professional" activity. You needed a computer, you needed to know how to operate it, you needed to know how to connect a cable. Publishing information was even more restricted — only institutions, media, and enterprises could do it. For ordinary people, going online meant reading news, looking up information, passively receiving.

Then something happened. Broadband spread. Computers became cheaper. Blogs, forums, and microblogs appeared. Ordinary people could post, write articles, and express opinions. The internet shifted from "information broadcast" to "information square."

The core of this decentralization: from only institutions publishing information, to everyone being able to see information and make their voice heard.

There was anxiety then too. Traditional media feared being replaced. Physical stores feared being crushed by e-commerce. But looking back, how many new professions did that decentralization create? Independent media, e-commerce, online marketing. The internet did not take away jobs. It changed them.

The second decentralization: expression and commercial capability.

What truly brought the internet into ordinary people's lives was the smartphone.

In the PC era, going online meant sitting in front of a computer. Then the mobile internet arrived. The phone was in your pocket, online anytime, anywhere. Ordinary people began to open stores with their phones, shoot short videos, do live streaming, take delivery orders. One person, one phone, could accomplish what previously required a team.

The core of this decentralization: from only enterprises being able to open stores and create, to everyone being able to become a creator, a merchant, a service provider.

The anxiety then was no less than today. Physical stores said e-commerce was stealing business. Taxis said ride-hailing was stealing jobs. TV stations said short videos were disrupting. But what was the result? E-commerce created countless small and medium sellers. Ride-hailing solved employment for tens of millions. Short videos made a large number of ordinary people famous. Anxiety was dissolved by enormous creativity.

The third decentralization: cognition and decision-making capability.

Now it is AI's turn.

## Insight

Today, AI capability is mainly concentrated in the hands of enterprises. Large companies use AI to write proposals, do design, analyze data, and optimize workflows. The AI ordinary people use is mostly a "question-and-answer tool" — ask a question, get an answer, use it and leave. It does not remember you. It does not belong to you.

The next decentralization is about putting cognition and decision-making capability into everyone's hands. Not giving you a stronger tool, but turning your memory, experience, and judgment into digital assets that can accumulate, be taken away, and grow more valuable the more they are used.

This is the **Third Cycle of Capability Decentralization [IR-TCC-005]**.

Enterprises have AI. You should have it too. What they can do, you can do too.

This is not about eliminating the gap. It is about filling the structural chasm of "having AI or not."

Look back. Every decentralization went through three stages:

Concentration: capability is held by a few. Ordinary people worry, "Will I be eliminated?"

Popularization: technical barriers drop, costs fall, ordinary people begin to access and use it.

Explosion: capability becomes infrastructure. Everyone uses it. New opportunities emerge.

The internet took about twenty years. The mobile internet took about ten. The AI era may be faster.

The rhyme of history tells us: every technological wave ultimately moves toward the decentralization and popularization of capability. AI is not an exception. It is another cycle.

This is why the **Second Brain [IR-SB-001]** matters. It is not a stronger AI. It is the mechanism through which cognition and decision-making capability become personally owned — a **True Exclusive AI [IR-TEA-002]** that can be taken away, that the platform cannot read, that does not break when compute is interrupted, and that remains accountable for every key decision.

The mechanism behind this is the **Compound Interest of Memory [IR-CIM-006]**. Every conversation, every correction, every expression of preference settles into your digital memory. Over time, your AI understands you better. It becomes the personal amplifier that turns one person's capability into something approaching a team's. This is the **Capability Parity Device** — not a weapon to overpower others, but a counterweight on your side of a tilting scale.

## Implication

Your anxiety is not because you cannot keep up. It is because your stage has not been built yet.

For individuals, this means the third decentralization is not about learning to use AI better. It is about owning your own AI — one that remembers your experience, accumulates your judgment, and belongs to you. This is what **Memory Sovereignty [IR-MS-006]** means in practice: view, export, migrate, delete, authorize. The bottom line is permanent memory and one-click forgetting.

For the industry, this means the value of AI shifts from selling access to enabling ownership. The platform that first provides a genuine personal AI — not a rented tool — will define the next era of user relationships.

For society, this means the third decentralization is the final piece. After information, after commerce, now cognition. When everyone has a personal AI, the **Ultimate Turn of Anxiety [IR-UTA-007]** will complete: from "fear of replacement" to "fear of losing." Fear makes people escape. Cherishing makes people embrace.

The internet took twenty years. The mobile internet took ten. The AI era will be faster. But the structure is the same. Concentrate, panic, decentralize, popularize.

The question is not whether the third decentralization will happen. It is who moves first.
