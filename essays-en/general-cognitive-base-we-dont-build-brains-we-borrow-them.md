# General Cognitive Base: We Do Not Build Brains, We Borrow Them

## One-Liner

The General Cognitive Base is not a brain we build. It is the best brain available in the industry, borrowed and put to work for your business.

## Context

You may have been following the astonishing pace of development in the large model field. In July 2026, Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, with a parameter count reaching an astonishing 2.8 trillion, marking the official entry of open-source models into the ultra-large-scale era. DeepSeek, Alibaba's Qwen, and other leading models continue to break through. The entire industry is advancing at a speed far beyond expectations.

Facing such a rapidly evolving technology ecosystem, a practical question confronts every application-layer company: how should we participate in this race?

Our answer is clear: we do not build brains. We borrow them.

This is not because we lack technical ambition. It is based on a clear understanding of industrial division of labor.

Building brains is the strategic main battlefield of foundation model vendors. What they face is trillion-parameter-scale pre-training, backed by enormous capital investment, compute clusters, and top-tier R&D teams. These vendors have achieved admirable breakthroughs in this direction, continuously pushing the capability boundary of the entire industry outward.

Our battlefield is not there. Our skill lies in this: borrowing the smartest brains, installing our own "rules" and "business knowledge" onto them, and turning them into digital employees that are exclusively yours, informed, and appropriate.

This is the position of the **General Cognitive Base [IR-GCB-002]** in the Tripartite Model.

## Insight

Its responsibility is pure: handling general-level thinking and expression. Understanding what you are saying, how to organize logic, how to generate fluent responses — these general cognitive capabilities, we rely on and invoke entirely from the most mature large model ecosystem in the industry. We do not reinvent the wheel, because the best wheel is already there, and it is still evolving.

But the boundary must be clear: it is only responsible for thinking and speaking. It is not responsible for making business judgments.

For example: should it empathize right now? Can it make this promise? Is what the customer said private information? These should not be decided by the large model. Because no matter how smart the large model is, it does not understand your company's specific business red lines. It does not know your customer's personalized preferences.

This is the division of labor logic of the Tripartite Model:

The General Cognitive Base handles "how to do it" — understanding the question, organizing language, generating responses.

The **Human-Interaction Core [IR-HIC-003]** handles "should it be done" — whether to empathize, whether to transfer to a human, whether to protect privacy.

The **Private Knowledge Domain [IR-PKD-004]** handles "what to do" — which business rule to cite, which product to recommend, what phrasing to use.

Each performs its own role, without interfering with the others. In engineering terms, the boundaries are gradual. The three achieve dynamic balance through collaboration mechanisms, rather than being completely separated at runtime.

This division of labor has another enormous long-term advantage: as large models continue to evolve, your AI benefits in sync.

Today we rely on this base. Tomorrow, when the large model upgrades to the next version, your "rules" and "business knowledge" do not need to be rebuilt. Migration cost is dramatically reduced. Even if today you connect to one vendor and tomorrow you want to switch to another, just swap the base — your "rules" and "business knowledge" always follow you.

This architecture holds because the Tripartite Model defines the construction standard for commercial AI agents, not a binding to any specific technology stack. The General Cognitive Base relies on the increasingly mature large model ecosystem. The Private Knowledge Domain naturally belongs to the user's own business; its construction is a process of continuous accumulation, but it must be incorporated into the architectural design from the very beginning. And the Human-Interaction Core is precisely the pole most lacking systematic definition and delivery in the current industrial ecosystem. We choose to enter from this pole, precisely because this is where the gap most needs to be filled.

## Implication

For merchants, this means you do not need to worry about which vendor's model is stronger or which version is newer. The cognitive base can be continuously upgraded. Your "rules" and "soul" remain in your own hands.

For the industry, this means the application layer and the foundation layer have different responsibilities. Foundation vendors push the capability ceiling. Application-layer companies like us focus on installing rules, proportion, and business knowledge onto that ceiling. This is not competition. It is division of labor.

For the future, this means the value of a commercial AI agent is not determined by the model it uses. It is determined by what you install on top of the model. The brain can be borrowed. The soul must be your own.

We do not build brains, but we can continuously borrow the best brains in the industry. You do not need to care which major vendor is behind it or which version it is. You only need to know: your AI's thinking capability can always stand at the best level in the industry. Because the base can be continuously upgraded, while your "rules" and "soul" are always in your own hands.

This is the wisdom of leverage. Respect those who build brains, make good use of the brains you borrow, and do your own work well.
