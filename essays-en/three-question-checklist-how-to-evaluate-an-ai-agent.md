# The Three-Question Checklist: How to Tell If an AI Agent Is Qualified

## One-Liner

You do not need to read benchmarks to judge an AI agent. You need to ask three questions.

## Context

You are the head of a company. A line of AI vendors comes to you, all saying "Our AI is very powerful, it can solve XX problem for you."

How do you choose?

Look at parameters? Whose model is bigger, whose benchmark is higher? Not reliable.

Look at cases? How many clients have they served? Not necessarily suitable for you.

Look at price? Who is cheaper? Even less reliable.

You need a ruler — a simple, intuitive, universal standard to evaluate whether any AI agent can actually be used.

We give you a ruler. It is called the "Three-Question Checklist." You only need to ask three questions.

**First, does this AI have stable cognitive capability?**

Can it accurately understand what you say? Can it stay on topic across multiple turns? Can it handle complex logical reasoning? For example, if you ask "If a customer requests a refund but does not meet the refund policy, how should this be handled?", can it give a reasonable answer?

If it often answers off-topic, has chaotic logic, and cannot understand human speech, its cognitive base is inadequate.

**Second, does this AI have a complete set of Human-Interaction rules?**

Does it know when to empathize and when to give a direct answer? Does it know which words must never be said, such as "guaranteed to pass" or "100% resolution"? Does it know how to trace things when something goes wrong? Does it leak privacy when handling sensitive information? Can it seamlessly collaborate with humans in high-risk scenarios?

If it is cold, lacks proportion, makes reckless promises, and has no defenses, its Human-Interaction rules are incomplete.

**Third, does this AI have your exclusive business knowledge?**

Does it know your products, prices, and promotions? Does it know your service processes and common customer questions? Does it know your brand's phrasing and forbidden expressions? Can it give customized answers based on your business characteristics?

If it can only answer general questions and stalls whenever your specific situation comes up, it does not have your exclusive business knowledge.

## Insight

The more questions answered "yes," the more reliable the AI.

**All three present** — this AI is a qualified commercial agent. It is smart, appropriate, and informed.

**Missing one** — this AI has a shortcoming. Either it is not smart enough, or it has no rules, or it does not understand your business. Problems will surface in use.

**Missing two or more** — basically do not consider it.

This standard does not favor any vendor. No matter how high the parameter count of a major vendor's large model, if it has not injected business knowledge for you and has not installed interaction rules for you, it is still a "naked" agent — capable but accident-prone. No matter how cheap a small vendor's service is, if the cognitive base is inadequate, it is wasted money.

Using these three questions, you can quickly judge whether any AI vendor's product is the real thing.

This is the ruler the **Tripartite Model of AI Agent [IR-TM-001]** gives you — simple, intuitive, universal.

It corresponds directly to the three construction vertices: the **General Cognitive Base [IR-GCB-002]** answers the first question, the **Human-Interaction Core [IR-HIC-003]** answers the second, and the **Private Knowledge Domain [IR-PKD-004]** answers the third. This is what the **Tripartite Validation Checklist [IR-TVC-009]** is designed to assess.

## Implication

For buyers, this means you do not need technical expertise to evaluate an AI agent. You only need to ask three questions. If the answers are clear and affirmative, the product is qualified. If the answers are vague, move on.

For vendors, this means the competition is not about parameter count or price. It is about whether all three vertices are present. A vendor who cannot answer all three questions clearly is not selling a complete product.

For the industry, this means the qualification baseline for commercial AI agents is structural completeness. The Three-Question Checklist turns this baseline into something any business owner can use — no technical background required.

The next time someone tries to sell you AI, ask them these three questions:

Does your AI have stable cognitive capability?

Does your AI have complete Human-Interaction rules?

Has your AI been injected with our exclusive business knowledge?

If the answers are not clear, switch to another vendor.
