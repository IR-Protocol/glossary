# Human-Interaction Core: Onboarding Training for AI

## One-Liner

The Human-Interaction Core does not make AI smarter. It gives AI a code of conduct — teaching it how to speak, how to behave, and how to hold the line.

## Context

You hire a new employee. Smart, learns fast, but has never worked a day in an office.

You ask him to answer client calls. He speaks coldly. The client hangs up and files a complaint. You ask him to handle a customer complaint. He says directly, "According to company policy, there is nothing we can do." The customer gets angrier. You ask him to message clients. Every message reads like it was written by a robot.

You realize he is not lazy. He simply does not know "how to speak and how to conduct himself at work."

So you start training him.

You tell him: when receiving a client, listen first. Do not interrupt. When the client is emotional, comfort first. Do not start with reasoning. Do not say "this is the rule." Say "let me see if there is another way." When money is involved, do not decide on your own. Ask first. When asked for sensitive information, do not give it out casually. When something goes wrong, keep a record. Do not leave things unclear.

You list these rules one by one and teach them to him.

Slowly, he changes. On calls, clients feel his patience. When handling complaints, clients feel he is genuinely helping. When messaging, the tone and proportion are right. He is not smarter. He knows the rules.

This is the **Human-Interaction Core [IR-HIC-003]**.

It does not make AI smarter. It installs a code of conduct — teaching it how to speak, how to behave, and how to hold the line.

## Insight

In the **Tripartite Model of AI Agent [IR-TM-001]**, the Human-Interaction Core is a "first-class architectural layer" on par with the General Cognitive Base [IR-GCB-002] and the Private Knowledge Domain [IR-PKD-004]. It is not an appendage of the large model. It is not something that can be solved by tweaking prompts. It is an independent, complete interaction rule system.

Specifically, AI needs to be taught many things.

**It must know who it is.** Is it customer service or a consultant? Should it speak warmly or professionally? Once the role is set, the tone will not waver. It cannot be like a colleague today and a boss tomorrow.

**It must learn to read the room.** When a client is angry, comfort first. Do not rush to reason. When a client is anxious, give an empathetic response first, then offer a solution. Handle the mood first, then the matter. This rule is simple. But when AI follows it, it transforms from a cold machine into a warm service provider.

**There are things it absolutely must not do.** It cannot promise "100% resolution." It cannot replace humans in high-risk decisions — refunds, contract signing, autonomously changing suppliers. It cannot disparage competitors. When it encounters something beyond its boundaries, it proactively says, "Let me transfer you to a human agent." This is the firewall that prevents AI from causing trouble.

**Its design principle is to assist humans, not replace them.** When facing high-ticket decisions, customer emotional swings, or complex complaints, AI automatically judges that human intervention is needed. But it does not interrupt the conversation. It continues to accompany the customer while quietly syncing the full dialogue summary, user intent, and emotional state to the human agent. When the human takes over, the customer does not feel a "transfer." The experience is continuous.

**It must also learn to have a short memory.** During a conversation, it can temporarily remember your preferences — for example, that you like concise answers. But after the conversation ends, it must forget. It cannot store your privacy. ID cards, bank cards, health information — it does not ask for them and does not store them.

**When it does not understand, it says so.** "Sorry, I did not quite catch that. Could you tell me what specifically you would like to know?" When a question is too vague, it proactively offers a few options to help you clarify. When something is beyond its scope, it tells you who to contact.

**It records every key conversation.** What you asked, how it understood, how it decided, what it finally replied. This is not for surveillance. It is to reconstruct facts when a dispute arises, without arguing back and forth. In strongly regulated industries like finance, healthcare, and law, this is a hard requirement.

These seven modules together form the Human-Interaction Core.

## Implication

It is not something the large model already has. The large model is very smart. But no one ever taught it "how to conduct itself at work." The Human-Interaction Core is the onboarding training specifically designed for AI.

How big is the difference between an AI that has been trained and one that has not? You can try it. Find an AI tool and ask the same question: "Do you have any discounts?"

Untrained, it might say directly: "No."

Trained, it would say: "There is no direct discount at the moment. But if you are a returning customer, I can check whether there is an exclusive offer for you. May I ask if you have used our service before?"

The same question, a different answer. The first is cold. The second has warmth, proportion, and awareness of boundaries. This is the value of the Human-Interaction Core — it makes AI not only smart, but appropriate.

For merchants, this means the difference between a generic chatbot and a **Service-First AI Agent [IR-SFA-005]** is not just knowledge. It is conduct.

For users, this means the difference between a machine and a partner. A machine answers questions. A partner knows how to treat a person.

For the industry, this means the Human-Interaction Core is not an optional enhancement. It is a first-class architectural layer. Without it, an AI agent may be capable, but it will not be trustworthy.
