# From Geometric Scaling to Cognitive Shrinkage
## What Huawei's "Tao (τ) Law" and Our Cognitive Architecture White Paper Have in Common

*We are Intention Resonance Technology. We write this because we see a pattern forming — across semiconductors and AI, China's tech industry is shifting from "single-point breakthroughs" to "system-defined architectures."*

On May 25, in Shanghai, Huawei introduced the **"Tao (τ) Law"** — a proposition that replaces *geometric scaling* with *temporal folding* to circumvent the physical limits of Moore's Law. It argues that when you can no longer make transistors smaller, you make signals travel shorter by folding circuits into three dimensions.

Two days later, in Songshan Lake, Dongguan, we released our *Cognitive Architecture & Cognitive Operating System White Paper* — proposing that when you can no longer make models larger without losing control, you restructure the decision pipeline so AI learns not just what to do, but *when and how* to do it.

One addresses signal delay in silicon. The other addresses judgment delay in cognition. Different domains, but the same underlying insight: **when a single dimension hits its limit, you must introduce a new dimension to rewrite the rules.**

For fifty years, the semiconductor industry focused on one thing: making transistors smaller. From tens of nanometers down to a few, the strategy was always *geometric scaling*. Today, both physics and cost say "no further."

Huawei did not double down on process node shrinkage. Instead, it stacked planar circuits vertically, shortening the physical path signals travel — trading *time* for *space density*. It is not acceleration; it is a lane change.

The AI industry is replaying the same script.

For three years, we have worshipped one metric: model size. Hundreds of billions of parameters to trillions. Short contexts to long ones. Benchmarks, clusters, FLOPs — all forms of *geometric scaling in the compute dimension*.

Yet a model that passes every professional exam still takes a trembling "I'm fine" at face value. An agent that consumes all human knowledge still responds to a midnight breakdown with a long, elegant, and utterly useless essay.

The problem is not capability. The problem is that capability has grown enormous, but no one taught the system what *propriety* means.

**Raw compute is muscle.** Without a skeleton and a nervous system, more muscle only means less control.

If Huawei is doing *temporal folding* at the physical layer, what we are proposing is *cognitive shrinkage* at the architectural layer.

Huawei reduces signal travel time by folding circuits in space. We shorten AI's cognitive path from *perception* to *appropriate response* by decoupling the pipeline.

Today's mainstream AI crams everything — perception, arbitration, scheduling, generation — into a single black box. When task and constraint conflict, the model negotiates with itself and produces a reply that is neither efficient nor warm.

*Cognitive shrinkage* splits these functions apart: each module does one thing well, and a dedicated scheduler coordinates them.

**Contextual awareness** — teaching AI to *read the room*.

Human communication is never just text. The same phrase — "I'm fine" — means the opposite depending on whether it is said with a smile or through tears. A mature system needs to perceive physical space, social attributes, time pressure, and emotional state. It must answer the question that today's AI consistently fails at:

**"What is going on right now?"**

**Independent judgment** — giving AI the authority to make trade-offs.

When emotional needs and task instructions pull in opposite directions, there must be an independent arbiter in the system. No compromise, no middle ground — a judgment based on the specific context.

If the user is emotionally breaking down, even the most urgent report must wait. If the user is in an emergency, efficiency comes first — cut every unnecessary word.

**Relational resource allocation** — knowing how much effort to give.

Today's AI applies the same compute budget to comforting a grieving person and checking tomorrow's weather. That makes no sense. Cognitive Operating System (COS) should make relationship states explicit and allocate resources accordingly. High relational intensity → more resources for delicate wording. Low relational intensity → straight answers, no detours.

**Evolution with a boundary** — the leash on self-improvement.

Technology can keep getting smarter, but the bottom line — what is right, what should be protected first, what cannot be compromised — must always remain under human oversight. Evolution is open. The red line is locked.

Huawei used logical folding to reclaim physical density.

We use cognitive architecture to restructure decision flow — pulling perception, arbitration, scheduling, and generation out of a single black box into separate yet coordinated layers.

Both are paradigm shifts: when a single dimension hits its ceiling, you elevate the problem to a higher dimension.

Huawei has proven that without unlimited process node shrinking, architectural innovation can still deliver performance leaps.

What we aim to prove is that without infinite parameter expansion, cognitive architectural innovation can move AI from *doing* to *knowing when*, and from *answering everything* to *knowing when not to answer*.

It is an interesting coincidence. Huawei has its terminal headquarters on the shores of Songshan Lake. We published our white paper from the same lake.

This lake seems to be a place for thinking about *structure* — not making things bigger, but making structures smarter.

On May 25, Huawei redefined the next curve for semiconductor evolution.

On May 27, we asked the next question for AI: as models become increasingly powerful, what determines whether they can be truly trusted is no longer their upper bound of capability — it is their lower bound of propriety.

Two paradigm shifts.
One in silicon.
One in cognition.

When the chip industry starts trading *time* for *space*, and the AI industry starts trading *parameters* for *architecture*, these signals together point to a single trend:

**China's technology sector is moving from "single-point breakthroughs" to "system-defined architectures."**
